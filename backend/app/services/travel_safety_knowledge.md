# DressApp Travel Safety and Cultural Etiquette Guidelines

This document serves as the official reference database for DressApp's Suitcase traveling AI assistant. It provides context on cultural conventions, religious dress codes, and danger zones where inappropriate attire can result in severe legal penalties or safety risks.

---

## Islamic Republic of Iran (Danger Zone)

### Legal Context & Penalties
* **Hijab Law**: The hijab is legally mandated for all women in public places in Iran, regardless of their nationality or religion.
* **Penalties**: Violating public modesty or hijab laws can lead to arrest, detention, heavy fines, court-martial/prosecution, or physical lashing. In extreme cases, confrontations with authorities can lead to severe harm or death.

### Women's Dress Code Requirements
* **Head Covering**: A headscarf (hijab or shayla) must cover the hair and neck at all times in public.
* **Manteau/Upper Body**: A loose-fitting coat or long tunic (manteau) must be worn. It must cover the torso, arms (at least three-quarter length, preferably long sleeves), and extend down to at least mid-thigh.
* **Lower Body**: Legs must be fully covered. Tight-fitting leggings or skinny jeans are strongly discouraged; wear loose trousers or a long skirt reaching the ankles.
* **Footwear**: Closed-toe shoes are recommended, though sandals are generally tolerated as long as the rest of the outfit is modest.

### Men's Dress Code Requirements
* **Trousers**: Men must wear long trousers. Wearing shorts in public is illegal and culturally unacceptable.
* **Shirts**: Short-sleeved shirts/T-shirts are acceptable, but sleeveless shirts (singlets or tank tops) are forbidden in public.

## Saudi Arabia (Modesty Zone)

### Legal Context & Etiquette
* **Public Decency Law**: The public decency regulation (2019) mandates that both tourists and residents dress modestly in public.
* **Abaya**: The traditional black robe (abaya) is no longer legally required for female tourists, but clothing must remain loose-fitting and cover shoulders and knees.

### Modest Dress Code Requirements
* **Women**: Arms and legs should be covered. Clothing must not be transparent, tight-fitting, or have offensive graphics. Head coverings are optional for tourists except when entering mosques.
* **Men**: Shoulders and knees must be covered. Shorts above the knee and sleeveless tank tops are inappropriate in public spaces.

## Vatican City & St. Peter's Basilica (Holy Place)

### Rules & Dress Code
* **Modesty Enforcement**: Guard staff strictly enforce the dress code at St. Peter's Basilica, the Vatican Museums, and the Sistine Chapel. Visitors who do not comply are denied entry.
* **Required Attire**:
  * **Shoulders**: Must be fully covered. No sleeveless tops, tank tops, or spaghetti straps.
  * **Knees**: Must be covered. No shorts, miniskirts, or dresses that sit above the knee.
  * **Headwear**: Men must remove hats/caps before entering the Basilica.

## Buddhist Temples in Thailand (Holy Place)

### Rules & Dress Code
* **Sacred Grounds**: Temples (Wats) such as Wat Phra Kaew (Temple of the Emerald Buddha) have strict modesty rules.
* **Required Attire**:
  * **Women & Men**: Shoulders, chests, and legs must be fully covered. Sleeveless shirts, crop tops, torn pants, shorts, or tight leggings are not allowed.
  * **Footwear**: Shoes must be removed before entering the inner sanctuary (ubosot). Wearing socks is acceptable.

## Shrines and Temples in Japan (Kyoto/Tokyo) (Holy Place)

### Rules & Dress Code
* **Attire**: Respectful and neat attire is expected. Extremely revealing clothes (crop tops, short shorts, low-cut shirts) are considered disrespectful.
* **Etiquette**: Remove hats and sunglasses inside temple halls. Remove shoes when entering indoor worship chambers (tatami rooms).

---

## Hindu Temples in India (Holy Place)

### Rules & Dress Code
* **Required Attire**: Modest clothing is mandatory. Women should wear outfits covering their shoulders, chest, and ankles (e.g., salwar kameez or long skirts). Men should wear long trousers and shirts.
* **Head Covering**: Some temples (especially Sikh Gurudwaras and certain Hindu temples) require visitors to cover their heads with a scarf or bandana.
* **Footwear & Leather**: Shoes must be removed. Leather belts, bags, or accessories are prohibited inside some shrines due to the sacred status of cattle.
