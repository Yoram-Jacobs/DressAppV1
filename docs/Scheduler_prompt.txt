**** New Stylist feature ****

*** The problem: Humans are facing 4 issues when planning an outfit:

1. Having a hard time composing outfits for special occasions. 
2. They are getting confused and unsure about composing different apparel daily. They tend to wear 3-4 sets of clothes over and over, neglecting the majority of their closet's assets.
3. Having a hard time remembering the outfit they’ve been wearing for a specific occasion.
4. Getting up in the morning without preparing the outfit the night before leads to hastily dressing, snapping the first garments they see on top of the stack, or even wearing yesterday’s outfit. 

- DressApp’s AI Stylist is to address them all with a new “AI Stylist scheduler”.

* The goal: Use all the closet assets and recommend sharing the unused items on the marketplace. *

*** The plan:

* Abstract:  “AI Stylist scheduler” is a Stylist page component. It has 2 workflows: ‘Scheduled’ and  ‘Event’. The  AI Stylist will manage all users' outfits in the “Outfits” page (new).

*** Workflowes:

** “Scheduled”: scheduled outfit proposal. Suggest 3 different outfits based on the user’s preset preferences. The Stylist sends a push notification to the user's phone on a scheduled time, reminding the user to get tomorrow’s outfit suggestions from the AI Stylist. The user can pick one or ask for a retry (suggest 3 other outfits). When the user picks an outfit, the Stylist saves it as a card to the “Outfits” page. 

* Preferences: 
1. Frequency: ‘Everyday’, Twice a week’, ‘Every other day.’ ‘On weekday’ (select the day).
2. Time: Notification time.
3. Style/Dress for: A dress code drop-down where the bottom line, ‘Dress for’  is a free text input, describing the demands, e.g., ‘Church’, ‘Gym’, ‘A hike’.

*** ”Event”: suggest an outfit for a special event. Suggest 3 different outfits based on the user’s prompt. The user can pick one or ask for a retry (suggest 3 other outfits). When the user picks an outfit, the Stylist saves it as a card to the “Outfits” page. The Stylist sends a push notification to the user's phone on a scheduled time, if set, reminding the user to get ready for the event. This workflow is the most user-sensitive; always use a real professional point of view. If the user doesn’t have any appropriate garments in their closet, search in the marketplace, too. Be honest and real - if there are no good matches, based on the prompt/event’s dress code and social demands, suggest the closest to preferences and constraints outfit, and recommend purchasing the least matching closet garments. Describe the garment that will best fit and offer the user assistance when selecting the garment in the store. 

* Similarities: When scaling the outfit constraints, never suggest the same outfit for a recurring event or for the same location. e.g., birthdays, parties, club. If there’s no other good match, suggest that outfit with a note “This outfit was in use on @date and place @place for @Event”.

* Preferences: 
1. Time: Notification time and date.

** General:

* Outfit suggestion: display the outfit on the user’s avatar as if the avatar is wearing it. 
* Outfit card (Outfits page): canvas displays the avatar wearing the outfit. Content: a special name, garment in the outfit, user’s prompt (Event), preferences, outfit’s usage; date, time, and location. 
* Rotation: always suggests different items. Keep track of the garment's usage date and rate. Try to suggest something never worn before, or garments worn long ago first. Keep track of the user’s rejections; if a garment was rejected 3 times, offer the user the opportunity to share  in the marketplace and free some closet space for more usable items. 
* Memory: Use the Outfit page as the local database for outfit inquiries. 
* Avatar: The Stylist will apply the avatar dressing for all outfit completion or suggestions.
